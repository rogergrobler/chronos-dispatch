TymeBank named in FT's Africa Fastest-Growing list — alongside OmniRetail, M-KOPA, Sabi, Thndr

Muyiwa Matuluko · Tue 12 May 2026

The Financial Times' 2026 ranking of Africa's Fastest-Growing Companies includes TymeBank in a 30-strong cohort spanning fintech, retail tech, and digital infrastructure. Thndr (Egypt) tops the list — the first time an Egyptian company has held the position. M-KOPA, OmniRetail, Sabi and Termii also appear.

Read at techcabal.com

The FT ranking is a useful external benchmark precisely because it cuts across sectors — fintech, retail tech, edtech, climate, logistics. TymeBank's inclusion at this stage of its lifecycle (post-Series D, multi-market, profitability inflection) is notable: most of the cohort are earlier-stage and growing off smaller bases. TymeBank's appearance signals durable revenue growth at scale.

Thndr taking the top spot matters too. Egypt has been outside the typical East/West Africa fintech narrative; a digital investment platform leading the FT list reframes which markets are absorbing capital efficiently. For our Lima Singapore narrative, this is exactly the kind of external evidence we should cite.

"The FT's Africa list is becoming the closest thing we have to a credible African growth benchmark." — TechCabal editorial

Suggest: forward this to the TymeBank IR team for use in their Q2 LP comms; revisit Thndr as a potential thematic adjacent investment in the Lima fund pipeline (assuming valuation is rational post-listing); track M-KOPA's energy-finance overlap as relevant to rain's customer-financing experiments.

Sequoia: "2026: This is AGI" — and the timing argument behind it

Sequoia Capital · 2026 perspective piece

Sequoia's flagship perspective argues that 2026 is the inflection year for AGI capability — defined by models that can reliably automate cognitive work across domains, not just chat. The implication: the venture playbook of the next five years is "buy the agent layer, sell the application layer". This is at tension with our deployment-economics framing.

Read at sequoiacap.com

Sequoia's argument: AGI in the practical sense (cognitive work automation, multi-step task execution, durable cross-domain capability) is here in 2026. Their bet structure follows from this — go heavy on the agent platforms (OpenAI, Anthropic, the integrators), light on the vertical applications because they'll be commoditised by the agents.

Our view from Issue 001's Fintech Brainfood take: AI capability is abundant; operating-model integration is the bottleneck. If that's right, Sequoia is over-paying at the platform layer and under-investing in the operating-model rebuilds that turn capability into cash flow.

"If everyone has the agent, the moat is in the workflow restructure around it." — Issue 001 framing, reinforced

Action: this is worth a 30-minute partner discussion before the next IC. Are we right that Sequoia is paying for the platform moat that's about to evaporate? Or are they right that the agent layer captures most of the value? The bet has implications for Vertice MedTech valuation, OfferZen positioning, and how we underwrite any AI-applied deal in the next 18 months.

Energy · Heatmap AM
This is the moment power operators acquire pricing power. rain's tower-energy thesis benefits directly. Hyperscalers will increasingly chase distributed power and behind-the-meter generation — exactly the gap rain has been building into.

Eversource CEO: "Not interested" in new data-centre development — won't take the grid hit

Heatmap AM · Tue 12 May 2026

Eversource Energy CEO Joe Nolan publicly said the utility is "not interested" in developing new server farms across its territory because doing so would "only drive up the price of energy" for its other customers. Heatmap's morning briefing also covers the Max Road Fire in the Everglades (5,000+ acres), an unseasonable cold snap in the Upper Midwest, and continued Northeast gas pipeline expansion (Williams, Enbridge).

Read at heatmap.news/am

Eversource's stance is significant because it's the first time a major US investor-owned utility has publicly rejected hyperscaler load growth on consumer-cost grounds. The political logic is obvious — residential rate-payers don't want their bills going up to subsidise Microsoft's AI training. But the commercial logic is sharper: utilities now have a credible reason to demand a price premium for net new data-centre load.

For data-centre operators (and the AI labs that need them), this means: either pay above-grid pricing, or build behind-the-meter generation, or move to jurisdictions with less rate-payer politics. All three lift demand for distributed power solutions.

"The age of cheap grid power for hyperscalers is ending. The age of bespoke power deals is here." — implication

For Chronos: rain's distributed tower-energy assets are positioned to participate in this. The bigger play is asking whether rain can extend its energy capability into a B2B offering for African data-centre operators (Teraco, Africa Data Centres, etc.) facing the same grid constraints. Worth raising at next rain board.

Read at dailychartbook.com

The LP question "why allocate to private African PE when public EM is doing X%" gets sharper: because public EM is now a concentrated bet on Taiwan and Korea tech, not the diversifier it used to be.

"MSCI EM is no longer EM. It's TSMC plus 700 other things." — investor commentary

Worth a one-pager from Lima to current and prospective LPs framing this. Could be a useful conversation-starter in the August fundraise meetings.

Today's Tip · AI craft for the partners

Drop multiple documents in at once

When you're working through a board pack, an IC paper, or a set of memos, don't paste them one at a time. Drag all the related files into Claude in a single message — the management commentary, the financials, the analyst notes, the auditor's letter. Claude reads them as one combined context and can compare across them. The "one document at a time" habit kills the value of having a model that reads everything together.

Try this with the next portco board pack

"Here are the board pack, the CEO commentary, and last quarter's IC memo for [portco]. Compare them. Where do management's claims contradict last quarter's view or the analyst notes? Flag the three biggest gaps with the evidence."
