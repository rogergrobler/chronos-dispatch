AI Frontier · Macro Measurement
Economists Anton Korinek (University of Virginia / Anthropic) and co-authors at the Bank of Canada have published a Peterson Institute paper this week arguing that the US AI economy is growing at 2,000+ percent per year on a quality-adjusted basis — but is essentially invisible in conventional GDP statistics. Their preliminary estimate of nominal US AI GDP is ~$250bn in 2025; quality-adjusted real growth ran at ~2,290% in 2024 and ~2,271% in 2025. Compute spending alone went from $37bn in 2023 to $90bn in 2024 to $219bn in 2025. The reason it's invisible: per-unit AI prices fall almost as fast as quality-adjusted output rises, so nominal revenues grow only moderately while underlying capacity more than doubles annually. For a finance ministry running ten-year revenue projections, the implication is that the labour-tax-base shock probability is materially underweighted.

UVA × Anthropic × Bank of Canada — the US AI economy is growing 2,000% a year and your GDP data can't see it

Jack Clark · Import AI 459 · Mon 1 Jun 2026 · PIIE working paper
PIIE working paper · Import AI 459 · Spock

The paper's three measurement frames matter for any LP / policy / portfolio conversation about the AI cycle: nominal compute spend (~2.4× per year, slow to lift conventional GDP); raw compute capacity (>200% per year, lifted by chip-generation efficiency gains); and quality-adjusted AI output (~2,290% in 2024, ~2,271% in 2025, when algorithmic progress and inference-price drops are factored in). The authors argue that the conventional measurement gap is structurally different from prior tech-mismeasurement episodes (semiconductors, internet) because for the first time the rapidly-improving sector is a substitute for labour, not a complement. A "windfall that cannot be seen cannot be shared". The three policy recommendations — AI satellite accounts, primary-data partnerships between statistics agencies / companies / academia, AI-capacity inputs into medium-term projections — are direct asks of every G20 finance ministry, including Treasury in Pretoria and MAS in Singapore. The South African macro picture should now be modelled with an AI-output adjustment line; the SARB's 2028 return-to-target glidepath (Issue 012) assumes a labour-market structure that the Korinek paper says is already breaking.

Import AI 459 — Jack Clark

Korinek's authorship matters. He is the Anthropic-affiliated economist most credible to the central-banking, treasury, and BIS-adjacent community. The fact that the Bank of Canada is a co-author is the institutional signature — this paper will be read by every central-bank research department by Friday. The structural argument is that any country running medium-term fiscal projections off conventional GDP measurement is materially under-pricing both the labour-substitution shock (downside) and the productivity / windfall opportunity (upside). Sovereign wealth fund design, labour-market policy, tax-base reform — all are being mis-calibrated against an AI economy that is already five to ten times larger than it appears in the print.

For Chronos: pair this directly with the Bain finding in today's item one. The two reads sit in productive tension. Bain says AI value-realisation at the enterprise customer level is behind schedule. Korinek says AI quality-adjusted output is ~23× per year. Both can be true if the AI economy is currently consumed at the inference / model-layer / infrastructure level, not yet propagated through enterprise workflows into the conventional revenue line. The implication for portfolio AI thesis: the producer-surplus is real and large; the consumer-surplus is the asymmetry that has not yet arrived. That is the gap to position into.

"A finance ministry running ten-year revenue projections off the conventional data will materially underweight the probability of a labor-tax-base shock — and will be correspondingly unprepared." — Korinek, Korinek, Mokyr & Pestova, PIIE, May 2026

For SA-specific macro modelling: the Stats SA CPI release on Wednesday 16:00 SAST is the next live data point, and the SARB's hawks are pricing a labour-cost-pass-through assumption that assumes a pre-AI labour structure. Two members of the MPC have just been handed an external-research justification for their dissent. The next SARB statement should be parsed for whether they acknowledge it.

For LP positioning: this is the paper to share with Lima Singapore and the GCC sovereign-wealth contacts as a frame for the next AGM. Africa & EM · Moat Doctrine
Samiat Akande — a respected data-protection specialist writing in TechCabal — argues that trust, not speed, is now the durable competitive moat in African tech. Users are sharing more personal data than ever (payments, identity, location, employment, health, behaviour) and have become measurably more conscious of where that data goes. The startup that treats privacy as a post-launch afterthought is now losing customer-retention and regulator-access optionality at a rate that is visible in the data. The "build fast, fix later" doctrine that defined the 2019-2023 African startup playbook is being decommissioned in real time. For Optasia and any Chronos position in the credit-data layer, this is now both an operational note and a thesis-defining frame.

TechCabal: trust, not speed, is becoming the real competitive moat in African tech

Samiat Akande · TechCabal · Mon 1 Jun 2026

Akande's three operative arguments: (1) the data context has changed — African users now share payment, ID, location, employment, health, and behavioural data with multiple operators per week, and are measurably more aware of where it goes; (2) the startup blind spot is treating privacy as a post-launch concern, when in fact weak governance creates onboarding friction, reputation damage, regulator-access failures, and breach-tail risk that materially compresses the LTV-to-CAC curve; (3) privacy should be a product feature, embedded in product architecture (consent design, data minimisation, access control) rather than legal-team afterthought. The conclusion is the thesis statement Chronos has been circling for two weeks: the companies that will define the next phase of African tech are the ones that make trust visible and tangible. Akande's frame is the cleanest available statement of the moat doctrine that ran through Anglada's Optasia "trust as infrastructure" comment (Issue 010), Paga's geographical-moat read (Issue 012 / Roger's note), and NALA's institutional-investor footprint (Issue 013). The four reads converge on one doctrine: in African digital infrastructure, trust is the moat.

Today's Tip · AI craft for the partners

Force the 2×2 — make Claude arrange the trade-off on a single grid

Almost every IC, board, or LP discussion eventually wants to be on a 2×2. The horizontal axis is one strategic variable (cost / time / geography / risk); the vertical axis is the other (return / quality / control / regulatory exposure). The four quadrants compress the entire opportunity set into one slide that even the most distracted committee member can read in fifteen seconds. Most partners use Claude to list the trade-offs. The higher-leverage move is to make Claude build the grid. The doctrine: ask Claude to choose the axes first, defend them, populate the four quadrants with concrete worked examples from the document, and identify which quadrant the deal at hand actually occupies. The output is forced clarity; the byproduct is that the axes Claude defends are often better than the ones you would have drawn yourself.

A worked two-prompt example

Prompt 1: "Read this Project [X] IC paper. Propose a 2×2 grid that captures the most decision-relevant trade-off facing the committee tomorrow. Pick the two axes you would defend, name what sits in each of the four quadrants using specific operators / sub-segments / scenarios from the paper, and tell me which quadrant Project [X] is in. Then tell me which quadrant the committee will likely think it is in, and the disconnect." Prompt 2 (the kicker): "Now write the single slide. Three lines of body copy under the grid. The body copy must answer: (a) what bet are we making, (b) what would falsify it within twelve months, (c) what is the size of the cheque if the bet is right." Two prompts, one slide, one decision. The 2×2 is the highest-bandwidth artefact in a partnership's working life — Claude builds it faster than any of us, and often more honestly.
