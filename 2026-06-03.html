Africa & EM · SA Fintech Doctrine · Crossfin / Optasia Partner on stage
Michael Jordaan took the keynote at the RMB Think Summit 2026 yesterday and named the African fintech doctrine in four crisp lessons: build for the device people already own, sit behind the bank / telco / wallet rather than try to replace them, treat the operating constraint as the moat, and keep the unit economics brutally lean. He walked through Clickatell, Optasia, MyPinPad, Bank Zero, Lesaka, Nile and Crossfin as worked examples. The Optasia data point is the cleanest summary of the thesis: more than $30 million distributed per day, more than 32 million loan transactions, roughly 40 US cents per loan, ~30 second approval, industry-leading 1.2% default rate. Crossfin is named explicitly as the consolidator — "in a fragmented market, consolidation can be as powerful as invention." Two Chronos portfolio companies, in the public articulation of the moat doctrine, by one of the partners.

Jordaan at the RMB Think Summit — "treat the constraint as the moat, not as an obstacle"

Neesa Moodley · Daily Maverick Business Maverick · Tue 2 Jun 2026
Daily Maverick · RMB Think Summit · Spock

Lesson 1 — build for the device people already own. USSD, SMS, WhatsApp. Clickatell as the worked example. Nile shopping inside WhatsApp where it already has 97% penetration in Kenya, 96% in SA, 95% in Nigeria. Lesson 2 — don't try to be the bank. Optasia as the credit-scoring brain behind the telco or lender on record. The economics of 40c loans only work because no bank infrastructure ever touches the transaction. Lesson 3 — treat the constraint as the moat. No credit cards forced mobile money. No high-end phones forced USSD banking. No POS terminals forced soft POS. The certification and integration work that competitors can't copy in a week becomes the durable moat. Bank Zero's mutual-bank-licence structure as part of the product. Lesson 4 — keep the economics radical. Anything depending on fat margin per transaction was never going to survive. Zaru as rand-stablecoin infrastructure for gig payouts, supplier settlement, energy micropayments. Lesaka built around the 44%-of-value-still-in-cash reality. And the closing line — Crossfin as proof that in a fragmented market, consolidation can be as powerful as invention. The four lessons are the unified articulation of the moat doctrine that has been forming across Issues 010-014 (Anglada's "trust as infrastructure", Paga's geographical moat, NALA's flow-share, Akande's trust-as-moat). It is now also Jordaan's framework, on stage, in print.

Apollo's Slok prints the chart — US business formation is now visibly distorted by AI-lowered start-up cost

Torsten Slok · Apollo Daily Spark · Mon 2 Jun 2026

Three structural reads. One — the macro-measurement gap closes. Korinek (Issue 014) said the AI economy is invisible in conventional GDP. Slok says it is visible in new-business-formation statistics. Both can be true. The Bureau of Labor Statistics applications data is the leading indicator; conventional GDP catches up with a 2-4 year lag. The investment implication: the labour-market shock is already underway, not pending. Two — the bidirectional read. Lower start-up cost means more firm formation (Slok's chart). Higher productivity in incumbent firms with deployed AI capability means lower headcount per unit revenue. The two effects do not net out in the same labour cohort — the start-up jobs are in different geographies, different skill profiles, different sectors than the displaced incumbent jobs. The transitional labour-market friction is large even if the net employment number is small. Three — the SA / SEA read-across. The Slok argument is US-centric, but the underlying mechanism (AI-lowered start-up cost) is geography-independent. SA's start-up formation has been chronically depressed for two decades; the same mechanism applied in the SA context implies a meaningful uplift to small-business density over the next 24-36 months. The Crossfin / Optasia / Yoco operating thesis depends on exactly this — the SME-density expansion. Slok's chart is the macro permission to model that uplift as a likely outcome rather than a hopeful assumption.

Today's Tip · AI craft for the partners

The Sceptic Test — make Claude argue against your own paper before you send it

Every IC paper, board memo, LP update, and recommendation note carries an embedded narrative — the writer's case for the thing they want the reader to do. The discipline that separates good papers from durable papers is whether the writer has already heard the strongest version of the counter-argument before the committee reads it. Most partners use Claude to polish their draft. The higher-leverage move is to make Claude attack the draft — sharply, professionally, from the strongest position the reader could occupy. The doctrine: paste the draft, name the specific reader role you want Claude to play (sceptical LP, dissenting partner, hostile credit committee, regulator on the other side of the table), and instruct Claude to produce the three strongest objections it can muster, ranked by how much they would damage the case if raised in the room. Then ask Claude what the paper should change to defuse each one before it lands.

A worked two-prompt example

Prompt 1: "Read this IC paper. You are the most sceptical partner in the room — the one who has seen ten deals like this fail. Your job is not to be polite. Produce the three strongest objections you can muster, ranked by how much they would damage the recommendation if you raised them in the committee. For each objection, write one paragraph as you would say it out loud — sharp, specific, supported by a fact or comparable from the paper." Prompt 2 (the real value): "Now, for each of those three objections, write the two-sentence revision to the paper that would defuse the objection before I get to it. Mark which section of the paper each revision goes into. The objective is to land the recommendation with the objections already dead on arrival." Two prompts. One stronger paper. The objections you would otherwise eat in the meeting are now resolved before the meeting begins. The version of the paper that survives the Sceptic Test is the only version worth circulating.
