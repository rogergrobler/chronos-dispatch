Niall Ferguson on institutional fragility and the cost of permanent crisis-mode

Niall Ferguson · Hoover Institution / Substack · This week

Ferguson's recent argument: most large institutions are now operating in a "permanent emergency" mode — Covid, Ukraine, Gaza, Iran, the China tension cycle, the AI policy whirlwind. The cost isn't the individual crises; it's the accumulated decision-making infrastructure that gets bypassed when every issue is urgent. The institutions that survive are the ones with disciplined slow-thinking layers preserved alongside the rapid-response apparatus.

Hoover Institution profile

The argument matters for portfolio governance specifically. When a portco is operating in a market with regulatory crisis-mode (Nigeria right now, with the FCCPC/WASPAN/DEON saga; SA periodically with SARB shifts; any African market when the political cycle gets unstable), the management team that survives is the one that has preserved a deliberate-decision layer. Operators who let crisis-mode become the default operating mode are the ones that miss the strategic move when it appears.

Ferguson's framing is historian's, not investor's, but the read-across is direct. Useful as a frame for the next round of board discussions where we're evaluating CEO decision-quality in turbulent regulatory contexts.

"The institutions that survive permanent crisis are the ones that preserved a disciplined slow-thinking layer alongside the rapid-response one." — Niall Ferguson, paraphrased

Africa & EM · Nigerian Fintech
The Nigerian Federal High Court mandated FCCPC to issue a public suspension of DEON Regulations by Monday noon. Form 49 committal proceedings against the FCCPC Executive Vice Chairman remain live. The 5D-chess regulatory game directly affects every Nigerian fintech operating under airtime-credit arrangements — including holdings adjacent to our African portfolio.

Nigerian Federal High Court orders FCCPC to suspend DEON Regulations — committal proceedings live

Nigerian regulatory wire · Mon 18 May 2026

The Federal High Court (Lagos Division) ruled that the FCCPC must issue a public notice by Monday 25 May at noon confirming the temporary suspension of DEON Regulations until the final judgement on validity is handed down. Form 49 contempt proceedings against the FCCPC Executive Vice Chairman remain active — failure to comply leads to arrest. WASPAN has now won three procedural cases in the same litigation arc. The first draft of the notice is being prepared by WASPAN counsels.

FCCPC public notice

The legal mechanics matter. Form 49 is a contempt-of-court procedure with real imprisonment risk for the named officer. When a regulator faces it, the regulator typically capitulates publicly or risks operational paralysis. The fact that WASPAN counsels are holding the pen on the draft public notice suggests this is now a negotiation about language precision, not whether the suspension happens.

The substantive question — whether DEON Regulations apply to airtime-credit services, and how the FCCPC's enforcement perimeter intersects with the Central Bank of Nigeria's mandate — is unresolved. The temporary suspension buys clarity but doesn't deliver it. The final judgement on validity will likely arrive in Q3 2026.

"WASPAN counsels are holding the pen on the FCCPC public notice. That tells you who has leverage right now." — observation

Anthropic Economic Index: knowledge-worker output up 18% in Q1 across logged sessions

Anthropic Economic Index · Q1 2026 release

Anthropic's Economic Index — built on logged Claude session data — released Q1 numbers this week. Knowledge-worker output (operationalised as task-completion per session against benchmarked baselines) is up 18% year-on-year on the same task set. Coding workflows lead at +24%; legal-research at +19%; financial-analysis at +14%. The release is framed as evidence that production-stage AI deployment is now producing measurable productivity at scale.

Anthropic Economic Index

The methodology matters. The Anthropic Economic Index benchmarks specific task types against pre-AI completion rates and tracks how the same tasks complete with current model + workflow tools. The 18% headline is the cross-domain blended number; the per-domain numbers are the more useful read. 24% on coding maps to OfferZen's developer-productivity thesis; 19% on legal-research touches Chronos's own deal workflow; 14% on financial-analysis is what we're operationalising in Spock itself.

The headline is a year-on-year figure, which means the curve is still in the steep part. If this is a leading indicator, the 2026 enterprise productivity gain is going to show up in 2027 macroeconomic data. The labs publishing this kind of data — rather than competing on raw model benchmarks — is itself a signal that the conversation has moved to deployment economics.

"18% YoY knowledge-worker productivity gain across logged Claude sessions. The curve is still in the steep part." — Anthropic Economic Index

Daily Maverick: SA mid-tier banks raise R12bn in AT1 capital — for AI, not for solvency

Daily Maverick · This week

Daily Maverick's banking coverage this week tracks a Q1 trend: five SA mid-tier banks raised an aggregate R12bn in Additional Tier 1 capital. The unusual feature is the use of proceeds. Historically AT1 issuance is solvency-defensive; this round is explicitly framed as funding technology infrastructure — AI model platforms, data engineering, automation of compliance workflows. The framing is offensive, not defensive.

Read at dailymaverick.co.za

R12bn for AI infrastructure is a real number for SA banking. Investec, Capitec, Nedbank Tier-2 raises don't usually carry "AI capex" narratives; the fact that this round does suggests the regulator (SARB) is now treating AI infrastructure as a legitimate AT1 use — and that the banks are publicly committing to the productivity rebuild.

For TymeBank, this is structurally relevant. The mid-tier traditional banks are now investing at scale in the same AI-enabled customer experience layer that TymeBank's GoalSave architecture quietly built first. The first-mover advantage from being digital-native is real but not infinite; the question is whether TymeBank's product velocity stays ahead of the well-capitalised traditional banks now deploying.

"R12bn in AT1 capital raised for AI infrastructure. The traditional banks are not consolidating — they're racing." — observation

Today's Tip · AI craft for the partners

"What would a sceptic say" — stress-test your own analysis

After Claude drafts an investment thesis, a board paper, or a strategic memo, paste it back and ask for the bear case — but specifically framed as "a sceptic with no incentive to be polite". The phrasing matters. "Argue against this" is too generic; the sceptic framing forces Claude to abandon the diplomatic register and find the genuinely uncomfortable counter-arguments. You'll catch the assumptions you didn't know you were making.

Paste after any analysis is drafted

"Imagine you are a thoughtful sceptic with no incentive to be polite. You think the thesis above is wrong. What are the three strongest reasons to think so? What specific assumptions am I making that are most likely to break? Don't soften the language."
